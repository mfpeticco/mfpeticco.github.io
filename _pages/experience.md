### SpaceX — Starship Structures Engineer
*May – August 2024 · Starbase, TX*

<div class="exp-photo">{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/experience/spacex.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true %}</div>

Between my time at JPL and starting my PhD, I spent the summer at SpaceX in Starbase, Texas as the responsible engineer for the Block 2 Starship payload door structure. The work moved fast because of a tight feedback loop: I iterated the structural design against manufacturability with the mechanical design engineers, wrote the work instructions for the technicians who built it, and folded their shop-floor feedback back into the design. I ran preliminary structural analysis in Abaqus through the ANSA/META package, and two full 24-foot door weldments were built during my time there. I also designed a tool for installing and temporarily fixturing both 16-foot payload barrel feedlines.

### NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory — Robotics Mechanical Engineer
*August 2023 – April 2024 · Pasadena, CA*

<div class="exp-photo">{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/experience/jpl.jpg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true %}</div>

I led the mechanical effort for a three-week field test of JPL's Exobiology Extant Life Surveyor ([EELS](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/robotics-at-jpl/eels/)) snake robot on the Canadian Athabasca Glacier, a terrestrial analogue for the icy moons of Enceladus and Europa. On site I handled mechanical repairs and modifications, set up electronic ground-support equipment, reconfigured hardware between runs, and guided the safe transport and handling of the robot.

Back in the lab, I built the supporting hardware that made that testing possible: a mobility-screw swapping gantry that cut downtime by 70%, an 8-foot vertical-climb testbed, and a lightweight, field-transportable stand for the 40 kg climbing robot. I also upgraded the ~2 m vertical-climbing snake robot for a ~50% reduction in power consumption while improving its climbing ability and reliability, formulated preliminary thermal concepts for a Mars-sample-tube-sized lunar sensor package, and stood up a system for cryogenic dynamometer testing of robotic actuators.
</div>

### NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory — Internships
*March 2022 – June 2023 · Pasadena, CA*

Before my full-time role, I interned across three JPL groups over a year and a half.

#### Robotics Engineering Intern — Extreme Environment Robotics (347C)
*September 2022 – June 2023*

Under Matt Gildner, I designed, built, and documented testbeds for the EELS project, which uses actuated screw profiles along its length to move across ice. I built a pair of parallel pivoting ice walls sized to fit a walk-in freezer for developing EELS's vertical-mobility algorithms, iterated a screw-mobility testbed for gathering data on Archimedes-screw locomotion, and designed an apparatus that lets one person swap the robot's mobility screws far faster than before.

#### Mechanical Engineering Intern — Electronic Packaging Group (356B)
*June 2022 – August 2022*

Under Albert Rieck, I learned Siemens NX and used NX NASTRAN to design and analyze a pull-test setup for proof-testing the strut bonds that hold up the Focal Plane Electronics board on ESA's Atmospheric Remote-sensing Infrared Exoplanet Large-survey (ARIEL) mission.

#### Mechanical Engineering Intern — Nondestructive Evaluation & Advanced Actuators (355N)
*March 2022 – May 2022*

My first JPL internship, under Yoseph Bar-Cohen, supported a proof-of-concept linear piezoelectric motor for extremely cold, dusty environments. I modeled the actuator, including hysteresis, in MATLAB and Simulink.
</div>

<div class="exp-photo">{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/experience/epirus.png" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true %}</div>

### Caltech CAST Lab — Summer Undergraduate Researcher
*June 2020 – April 2021 · Pasadena, CA*

<div class="exp-photo">{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/experience/cast.jpg" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" zoomable=true %}</div>

Through Caltech's SURF program, I worked with the Center for Autonomous Systems and Technologies on extending satellite lifespans through in-orbit refueling. I designed two systems to demonstrate fuel transfer between the lab's 3-DoF spacecraft simulators, then derived and simulated the time-varying moment-of-inertia tensor in MATLAB so a spacecraft's controller could account for the shifting mass during transfer. My [final presentation](https://youtu.be/csShpmsHiCY) and [final report](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1wClyt1N715w9stv7HBsUqoqIrPaZEwS0/view?usp=sharing) go into more detail.
